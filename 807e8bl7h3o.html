(i) Constant load of 1000 virus particles per droplet We consider two droplets corresponding to the bimodal distribution with the corresponding geometric mean sizes of 72 µm and 386 µm for the two modes(3), with 1000 virus particles inside. The droplets are ejected into quiescent air at 50 m/s (as commonly reported for sneezing) at an average relative humidity of 65%, common in the UK and an ambient temperature of 50°F (10°C). The time taken for the droplets to evaporate to their ultimate size is calculated(8), considering no internal resistance to mass transfer and drying rate controlled by the external mass transfer film. Water is considered as the evaporating material and assuming that the presence of the virus does not hinder the drying of droplets. The drying rate of the two droplets sizes is shown in Figure 1. The evaporation time is 20 and 510 s for the two droplet sizes, respectively, and the ultimate cluster sizes in both cases are around 1µm. Considering a smaller virus loading of 100 viruses, the ultimate size will be obviously smaller and is around 0.46 µm.
